AI tools for graphic designers: a practical comparison guide

Explore design assistants, image generators and creative software, and learn how to choose tools that support rather than replace your design process.

By DigitalPickHub Editorial Team · Published 1 November 2026 · Research-led introductory guide

What should a designer look for?

The right tool depends on the work: social media graphics, brand identity, photo editing, illustration, presentation design or client campaigns. A useful AI feature should reduce repetitive work while leaving you in control of layout, typography, accuracy and final quality.

Tools worth comparing

  • Canva: useful for template-based design, social posts, presentations and quick visual content. Its AI features and usage limits depend on the current plan.
  • Adobe Firefly: supports generative image and creative workflows within Adobe’s ecosystem. Check the terms for the specific feature and plan.
  • Adobe Photoshop: a professional image-editing application with AI-assisted features. It offers more control but has a steeper learning curve than simple template tools.
  • Figma: a collaborative design platform for interface and product design. Explore its current AI capabilities and plan restrictions.
  • Leonardo AI: useful to investigate for generated visual concepts and assets. Verify licensing and output rights before client use.
  • Kittl: a design platform to explore for typography-led graphics, illustrations and merchandise-style layouts.

How to compare them fairly

Use the same brief in each tool. For example: create a square social post for a fictional bakery promotion, with a clear headline, one product image and a call to action. Compare:

  • How quickly you can reach a usable first draft.
  • Whether text renders correctly and can be edited.
  • How much control you have over spacing, colours and typography.
  • Whether you can export the file in the format you need.
  • Whether the plan permits the intended commercial use.

AI output still needs design judgement

Generated layouts can contain awkward spacing, inconsistent branding, incorrect text or visual details that do not make sense. Check every design at phone size and full size. Use licensed fonts, images and brand assets, and never assume an AI-generated image is automatically exclusive or cleared for every use.
